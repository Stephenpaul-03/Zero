# Japanese Writing Systems

Japanese uses three scripts together. They are not three separate alphabets; each has a different job in a sentence.

## Hiragana

Hiragana writes grammatical endings and particles, and can write native words. Learn to recognize and write the basic kana before leaning on romaji. See the [hiragana chart](/JapaneseN5-hiragana).

## Katakana

Katakana represents the same basic sounds as hiragana. It is commonly used for loanwords, many foreign names, sound effects, and emphasis. See the [katakana chart](/JapaneseN5-katakana).

## Kanji

Kanji are characters used for word roots and meanings. A kanji can have more than one reading; the reading depends on the word and context. Learn words together with their kana readings rather than memorizing an isolated character as if it had one fixed pronunciation.

## How they work together

In 私は日本語を勉強します (わたしは にほんごを べんきょうします), kanji carry much of the content, hiragana marks grammar and endings, and the word 日本語 is read as a complete vocabulary item. Real Japanese writing does not normally put spaces between words.

## First steps

- Read kana aloud in short daily sessions.
- Learn dakuten and handakuten (が, ぱ) and the small ゃ/ゅ/ょ combinations.
- Notice the small っ, which marks a brief pause or doubled consonant in many words.
- After kana, add kanji through vocabulary and short sentences.
