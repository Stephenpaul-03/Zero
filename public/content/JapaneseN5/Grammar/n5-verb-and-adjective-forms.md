# N5 Verb and Adjective Forms

This page is a compact map of common beginner forms. The workbook’s [N5 verb list](/JapaneseN5-verbs-reference), [i-adjectives](/JapaneseN5-i-adjectives-reference), and [na-adjectives](/JapaneseN5-na-adjectives-reference) give words to practice with.

## Polite verbs

A common polite pattern uses the ます stem:

| Form | Example: 読む (to read) |
| --- | --- |
| Non-past affirmative | 読みます — read / will read |
| Non-past negative | 読みません — do not / will not read |
| Past affirmative | 読みました — read (past) |
| Past negative | 読みませんでした — did not read |

Japanese non-past forms can refer to habits or future events; time words and context clarify which.

## て-form

The て-form connects actions and appears in requests and other patterns: **見てください** (please look), **食べて、行きます** (I will eat and then go). Conjugation depends on the verb group, so learn each form with examples.

## Adjectives

い-adjectives commonly change their final い: 高い → 高くない (not expensive/high), 高かった (was expensive/high). な-adjectives use な before a noun (静かな町, a quiet town) and commonly use です in polite statements (町は静かです).

Do not apply the い-adjective rule to every word ending in い; some words are な-adjectives or nouns.
