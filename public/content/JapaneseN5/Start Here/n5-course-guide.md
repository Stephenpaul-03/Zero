# Introduction to Japanese — N5

This workspace starts with the Japanese writing systems, sentence building, and the vocabulary and kanji marked N5 in the supplied workbook. The workbook also contains N4 entries; those are separated into the N4 sections so the levels stay distinct.

## A practical study order

1. Learn hiragana, then katakana. Read the [writing systems guide](/JapaneseN5-japanese-writing-systems).
2. Build simple sentences with the [N5 grammar guide](/JapaneseN5-n5-sentence-basics) and [particles](/JapaneseN5-n5-particles).
3. Study vocabulary in small groups and review kanji in context.
4. Move to the N4 section when N5 material feels familiar. N4 revisits the same scripts while adding more kanji, vocabulary, and grammar patterns.

JLPT levels are commonly used study guides; the JLPT does not publish a definitive official list of every required word, kanji, or grammar point. Treat workbook labels as the source for the reference tables and the authored lessons as a learning path.

## What is in the workbook

The spreadsheet includes separate tabs for kanji, nouns, verbs, katakana words, particles, adverbs, and adjective types. Every workbook row labeled N5 or N4 has been moved into the matching level and topic. The original spreadsheet is unchanged.
