# What is Echo?

Echo is a fullscreen Android companion for music that is already playing on your device. It gives the current track a dedicated visual space instead of asking you to keep opening the original music player.

## The idea

Most music players already know what is playing, which track is next, what artwork belongs to it, and which playback actions are available. Echo does not try to replace that knowledge. It connects to the active media session and presents it in a calmer, more visual interface.

The result is a dashboard focused on the moment of listening:

- large album artwork;
- title, artist, album, and progress information;
- a cover-flow view of nearby queue items;
- play, pause, previous, next, and seek actions when supported;
- volume controls for compatible sessions;
- light, dark, and system-aware presentation; and
- an immersive layout that works especially well in landscape.

## What Echo is for

Echo is useful when a device is acting as a dedicated music screen, when the original player is visually busy, or when you want the current track visible from a distance. It is intended to complement a player such as Poweramp rather than compete with it.

## What Echo is not

Echo is not a streaming service, music player, downloader, library manager, music store, or replacement for the connected media player. It does not provide songs, manage subscriptions, or own the queue. The other app remains responsible for the audio.

## Current project scope

The current Echo project is Android-focused and uses a custom native media-session module with a React Native interface. It has no Echo account, cloud library, analytics system, advertising network, payment flow, or music upload flow.
