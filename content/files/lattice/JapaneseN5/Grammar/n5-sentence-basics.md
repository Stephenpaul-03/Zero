# N5 Sentence Basics

Japanese usually places the verb at the end of a clause. Particles follow words or phrases and show their role. Subjects are often omitted when the context makes them clear.

## A simple statement

**わたしは がくせいです。**
*Watashi wa gakusei desu.* — I am a student.

は marks the topic. です makes the statement polite. In は, the written character is pronounced *wa* when it is the topic particle.

## Verb sentences

**わたしは みずを のみます。** — I drink water.
**きのう みずを のみました。** — I drank water yesterday.

を marks a direct object. The verb changes for tense and politeness; Japanese does not change the verb for each person.

## Questions

Add か to a polite question: **これは なんですか。** — What is this? In casual speech, intonation and context can mark a question without か.

## Existence and location

います is used for people and animals; あります is used for things and plants in the basic existence pattern.

- **ねこが います。** — There is a cat.
- **つくえのうえに ほんが あります。** — There is a book on the desk.

## Word order is flexible, particles are important

Time and location phrases can move, but the verb normally stays at the end. Particles help show what each phrase does. Start with short sentences and change one element at a time.
